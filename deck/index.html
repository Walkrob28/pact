2

Pact listens, and writes them down for you

Not a meeting summary. Not a transcript. Specifically the obligations: who owes what, by when.

It knows a promise from a request

"I'll send the quote Friday" is a commitment. "Can you send me the quote?" is not. Pact only tracks what you owe.

It hears the deadline

by Friday · tomorrow · within 48 hours · next Tuesday · end of week — extracted and attached.

It flags the soft ones

"I'll try to get to it" is scored lower and tagged hedged — so you know which promises are real.

It names the owner

Speaker diarization puts each commitment against the person who actually made it.

3

Two passes, because speed and accuracy aren't the same problem

AssemblyAI streaming gives turns, not identity. Diarization is async-only. That constraint shaped the architecture.

Live pass

Streaming WebSocket (v3). Commitments appear on screen mid-sentence, marked attribution pending — we never guess who spoke.

→

Close-out pass

Async transcription with speaker_labels re-runs the audio and puts a real name on every promise.

Live speed during the call. Accurate attribution the moment it ends.
